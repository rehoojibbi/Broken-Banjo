# Broken Banjo – Opening Chapter Outline (draft 1)

## Characters
- **Dave** – failed crime novelist. Eleven detective books, nobody reads them. Treats real life like one of his plots, is usually wrong in funny ways, but really does notice small details. Makes dry remarks to himself.
- **Viktor** – Dave's old friend, secretly a real spy. Kidnapped in the opening.
- **Victoria** – Viktor's daughter. Held captive somewhere. Clever and brave.
- **Officer Plumb** – front desk officer. Slow, eating a sandwich, obsessed with his crossword.
- **Gnome thief ("Big Terry")** – huge, soft-hearted, arrested for stealing garden gnomes. Secretly a genius. His reading glasses were confiscated as "potential weapons", so he can't finish his book on quantum physics.
- **Posh pickpocket ("Reginald")** – polite, vain, steals out of habit. Also secretly a genius. Always arguing with Terry.
- **Detective Harrow** (old, tired) and **Detective Finch** (young, smug).
- **The three men** – the kidnappers. One has a tattoo, one wears odd shoes.

---

## Cutscene – The Café (made by Andreas)
Dave sits at his café table, stuck on book twelve. The waiter comes to take his order, and while Dave is distracted, Viktor quietly swaps his newspaper for Dave's. Viktor leaves, steps into the street, gets hit by a dark car with a dented door, and three men drag him inside. The car drives off.

**What the player should remember (used later in the detectives' quiz):** the dent on the car door, the tattoo on one man's hand, and odd shoes on another.

## Room 1 – Police Station, Front Desk (first playable room)
**Setup:** Dave rushes in, shaken. Officer Plumb sits behind a glass window doing the crossword. Two cells hold Terry and Reginald, who are bickering.

**Goal:** get Officer Plumb to listen.

**Officer Plumb:**
- "I will not lift a finger until I've finished this crossword. One word left. It's been three days."
- The last clue: *"The act of judging something as worthless (29)"*. The answer is **FLOCCINAUCINIHILIPILIFICATION**. It's almost impossible to solve without the criminals' help, and even Dave, with eleven crime novels behind him, has never heard of it.
- Plumb: "Twenty-nine letters. Twenty-nine!"
- He keeps mumbling wrong guesses in the background: "Uselessness? Too short... Pointlessnessness? ...Tuesday?"

**The two favours (one clue each):**
1. **Terry's reading glasses.** Terry wants his reading glasses back so he can finish his quantum physics book. They're in the evidence box on the counter (a hotspot), labelled "potential weapon". Plumb stops you when he's looking. Dave has to wait until Plumb bends down to pick up a dropped pen, or distract him (e.g. ring the service bell and then grab the glasses). Give the glasses to Terry.
   - Terry's clue: "It's Latin, innit. Four old words for 'worthless': *floccus, naucum, nihil, pilus*. Stick 'em together and add *-ification*. Obvious, really."
2. **Reginald's argument.** Reginald and Terry are arguing about whether a tomato is a fruit. Reginald wants Dave to settle it.
   - Pick "fruit" and Reginald is delighted.
   - Pick "vegetable" and Terry is delighted, but Reginald sulks and says "Try again, old boy." You can ask again.
   - Reginald's clue: "Eton, 1987. The longest non-technical word in the Oxford dictionary. It starts with F, old boy, and it's twenty-nine letters. I counted on my fingers. Other people's fingers, mostly."

**Solving it:** once Dave has both clues, he talks to Plumb and picks the right word from a short list (other options: FLABBERGASTINATION, FLOCCIPAUCIPILIFICATION, and "FLOPPYDISKIFICATION"). If he picks wrong, Plumb says "That's not twenty-nine letters, sir" or "That's not a word, sir. I'd know. I've been at this three days."
- When he's right, Plumb fills it in, sighs happily, and presses the buzzer: "Detectives are through there. Don't touch anything."

**Dave's remarks (examples):**
- Looking at the cells: "Two criminals, one cell. Classic buddy-cop setup. Minus the cops."
- Looking at Plumb: "A man at peace with doing absolutely nothing. I envy him."
- Looking at the crossword on the counter: "Same paper as mine. Small world." *(A hint for later, not a puzzle.)*
- After the clues: "I've written eleven crime novels and never once used that word. Two men in a cell just taught it to me. I need a new career."

## Room 2 – Detectives' Office
**Setup:** Harrow and Finch are playing darts / eating doughnuts. They agree to "take a statement."

**The detail quiz (noticing puzzle):** they ask three questions. Each has 3–4 dialogue answers.
1. "The car. Anything special?" Right answer: **a dent in the driver's door.** Wrong answers: "It was fast.", "It was car-shaped."
2. "The men. Any marks?" Right answer: **a tattoo of a snake on a hand.** Wrong answers: "Moustache, probably.", "They looked... villainous."
3. "What were they wearing?" Right answer: **one had shoes that didn't match.** Wrong answers: "Hats. Spy hats.", "Clothes, mostly."

**The result:** whatever Dave answers, they laugh.
- Finch: "Car, tattoo, mismatched shoes. That's chapter one of your next book, isn't it, Mr. Novelist?"
- Harrow: "We've got better things to do. Send us a signed copy."
- **If Dave got all three right:** Harrow quietly hands him a business card on the way out: "If you see the car again... call me. Not him." *(Bonus item for later.)*

Dave leaves defeated: "Eleven books, and they still don't believe me. Twelve, if you count this."

**Exit:** back through the front desk. Terry and Reginald wave sadly, and Plumb doesn't even look up. Out to the street.

## Room 3 – The Street Outside the Station (evening)
**Setup:** it's raining a little. Dave stands outside, holding Viktor's newspaper, which he still thinks is his own.

**The choice:**
- **A) Walk home.** Dave: "A hot bath. A cold beer. Then bed, and forget all of this." This goes to the Victoria scene, then **Dave's apartment, next morning (he wakes up).**
- **B) Go to the park and do the crossword.** Dave sits on a bench under a streetlamp and opens the paper. He notices the crossword is already filled in, in pen: "Nobody does a crossword in pen unless they're showing off or hiding something." This is where the player solves the **hidden message puzzle**. Dave then goes home and doesn't sleep. This goes to the Victoria scene, then **Dave's apartment, the same night (awake, wired, the message already known).**

## Short Playable Cut – Victoria
**Setup:** a dark room (a basement or a boat's hold). Victoria is tied to a chair. Muffled voices of the three men are behind the door.
**Goal (tiny puzzle, 2–3 clicks):** she uses something nearby, like a nail sticking out of the chair, to loosen the rope a little, and overhears a key fact through the door (e.g. "Viktor won't talk. Bring him to the airport on Saturday.").
She doesn't escape yet, but she's clearly clever: "Rule one, Dad. Always listen at the door."
Cut to Dave.

## Dave's Apartment (the next scene, which leads into the existing demo rooms)
- **Path A (woke up):** Dave finds the newspaper on the kitchen table, notices the pen crossword, and solves the hidden message here as the first puzzle of the day.
- **Path B (didn't sleep):** Dave has already solved the message and is pacing around with coffee. The scene opens with him already knowing the meeting place, and there's extra dialogue ("I haven't slept. I've had four coffees. I can hear colours.").
- **Both paths:** the hidden message says something like "D – they found me. Victoria is in danger. Help her. Find the banjo." Dave: "The banjo? Viktor couldn't play the banjo. ...Could he?"

This leads into the street/airport part of the graybox you already have (with Rosa, the watcher, and so on) or its reworked version.

## Viktor's Crossword (undecided)
Viktor's newspaper crossword is already filled in and hides a puzzle the player can solve on their own. What exactly it reveals, and when, is still open. The ideas below are options only.

Options:
- It's a side puzzle the player can solve any time, and solving it early unlocks extra dialogue or an easier path.
- It's Viktor's backup message, so the "Find the banjo" hint can come before the letter.
- It looks solved, but some squares are deliberately wrong, and the wrong letters spell the real message.

## The Hidden Crossword Message (old idea, may change)
- The answers Viktor filled in look normal, but the **first letters of the circled answers** spell out the message.
- Dave's funny wrong first theory: he reads only the across answers and gets "FLORIST", and is convinced it points to a flower shop. The player has to look at the down answers too.
